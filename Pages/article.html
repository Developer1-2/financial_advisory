What is Compound Interest?

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original money (the principal) and on the interest that money has already earned. In simple terms, it is “interest on interest.”

This is different from simple interest, which is calculated only on the original amount. Over time, compound interest can cause your money to grow much faster than simple interest.

“Compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world. He who understands it, earns it; he who doesn’t, pays it.” — Often attributed to Albert Einstein

How compound interest works

When you invest or save money in an account that pays compound interest, the interest is added to your balance at regular intervals (daily, monthly, or annually). The next time interest is calculated, it is based on the new, higher balance.

The more frequently interest is compounded, and the longer you leave the money invested, the greater the effect.

A simple example

Imagine you invest $1,000 at an annual interest rate of 8%, compounded once per year.

  • Year 1: You earn $80. New balance = $1,080
  • Year 2: You earn 8% on $1,080 = $86.40. New balance = $1,166.40
  • Year 3: You earn 8% on $1,166.40 = $93.31. New balance = $1,259.71

After 10 years, that $1,000 would grow to about $2,159. After 30 years, it would grow to roughly $10,063 — more than ten times the original amount — without you adding any extra money.

The compound interest formula

The standard formula for compound interest is:

A = P (1 + r/n)nt

  • A = Final amount
  • P = Principal (starting amount)
  • r = Annual interest rate (as a decimal)
  • n = Number of times interest is compounded per year
  • t = Number of years

You don’t need to memorize the formula. Most investment platforms and online calculators will do the math for you. What matters is understanding the principle.

Why it matters for building wealth

Compound interest rewards two things above all: time and consistency. Starting early, even with small amounts, usually beats starting later with larger amounts.

This is why long-term investing in assets that grow over time (such as broad stock market index funds) is one of the most reliable ways ordinary people build significant wealth.

Key takeaways

  • Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money and previously earned interest.
  • Time is the most important ingredient — the longer your money compounds, the more powerful the effect becomes.
  • Even modest amounts can grow substantially over decades.
  • Understanding compound interest helps you make better decisions about saving, investing, and debt.
